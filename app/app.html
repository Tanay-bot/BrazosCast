        Dry road, no way out

        —

          Where the numbers come from. Live stage and official forecast: NOAA NWPS RMOT2 (USGS 08114000), action 20 ft · flood 45 ft · moderate 48 ft · major 50 ft. Per-street flood thresholds: NOAA-OWP HAND FIM (hand_fim_4_9_9_0/12070104), mainstem levelpath. Crest lead times: derived in-repo by cross-correlating historical gauge peaks (pipeline/analyze/travel_time_subdaily.py). Way out: computed in-repo from the road graph (pipeline/derive/isolation.py) — the stage at which every dry route off a street is cut. If RMOT2 stops reporting, the stage is estimated from the upstream gauges (pipeline/derive/gauge_redundancy.py), labelled as estimated with a measured uncertainty band — and only once the last observation is too old for that to be the better answer. Routing: shortest path on that same graph to real OpenStreetMap destinations — hospitals, fire and police stations (pipeline/derive/routing.py), flooded segments weighted rather than removed so a fully-cut network still reports the best crossing. Island: the same graph's connected pieces at the current stage — the roads still reachable from your street without crossing water — with the flooded segments at that island's edge drawn on the map. The extract is fragmented before any flooding, so an island without a responder is usually the boundary of the study area rather than a cut-off. Harvey backcast: the observed 2017 gauge record replayed through the same rule (pipeline/derive/backcast.py) — what the app would have sent, with the onsets and the crest observed and the per-street timing not. Flood zones: FEMA NFHL. Claims context: OpenFEMA.
          Cross-check: the upstream gauges (HPDT2, BAWT2) transferred down the reach at the lag each was fitted at, compared with the live reading against the error band measured on held-out events (pipeline/derive/gauge_redundancy.py). Robustness: the answer re-run with the per-street threshold error and the benchmark's forecast RMSE applied (pipeline/derive/ensemble.py, pipeline/models/forecast.py). Model cross-check: that benchmark's own record — xgboost against persistence at +1…+7 d on the 2016–17 holdout and two later windows, with threshold exceedance at the stages above. It beats persistence to +3 d and does not beyond, which is why its error is used as a spread and never as the forecast. Dry road, no way out: the same graph's segment_route_cut_ft, joined to each street's own threshold. Past stages: the USGS annual peak record for 08114000 (pipeline/derive/gauge_history.py) — annual maxima, not a continuous series. A forecast you paste is yours: it never leaves the page and is not part of any published number.

          A working prototype. Road depths are modelled, never observed. Flood extents are provisional pending a unit check on the HAND raster. A refuge is ground the modelled water does not reach at 50 ft — not a shelter — and routes leaving the mapped corridor are not counted, so a street marked “no dry route out”, or one with no modelled route to a responder, may still have one outside the study area. Destinations are volunteer-mapped OSM points, not a dispatch feed. 12 inches of moving water carries most cars away — never drive through water.
